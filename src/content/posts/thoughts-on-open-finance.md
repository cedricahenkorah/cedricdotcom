for a long time now, I've been thinking about open finance (some call it open banking) and how it could work here in Ghana. at its core, open finance means letting people securely share the financial data their banks and other institutions hold about them with third-party providers or other institutions, with their consent. that's hard to do in our part of the world right now, because the systems needed to make it work don't fully exist yet.

my first direct encounter with open finance came in 2024, at a company I used to work at. we had to integrate an open banking provider from the UK into one of our services, and I found the whole experience fascinating. it was an eye-opener to see how easily a user could consent to sharing their financial data from the different accounts and institutions they used. a technology like that opens up endless possibilities. later that year, the Bank of Ghana released a draft of its open banking directive, so I was happy to see we were exploring it. nothing significant has happened since that draft, though.

open finance lets us imagine a whole new kind of finance for businesses and individuals alike. most Ghanaians have financial data scattered across different institutions (MoMo accounts, banks, credit institutions, etc.), with little visibility into how it ties into their everyday life and work. most of these institutions have also been reluctant to build the trust and support needed to make this possible. I believe that gap can be bridged someday. I'm not entirely sure how, but I want to be in the space that makes it happen.

I'm working on something new called [Maton](https://maton.co), and we want to build the infrastructure that supports open finance for everyone. we believe everyone, individuals and businesses alike, needs a trusted way to access and control their financial data and grow their finances.
